What it is

NacoSim is a small rate-mode quad simulator that runs on the radio itself — an EdgeTX Lua script, no computer or cable needed once it is installed. You fly it with your real sticks and your own Betaflight rates: a wireframe view, a race track with gates, a lap timer and a beep on every gate. The picture is crude (it has to fit a 128×64 black and white screen); the point is stick feel and rates.

Developed and tested on a Jumper T14 with EdgeTX 2.10. Other radios are untested; there is a short list of things worth checking below.

Install

  1. Download the zip above and unzip it. You get a SCRIPTS folder and a README.
  2. Put the radio in USB Storage mode (or take the SD card out) so the SD card shows up as a drive on your computer.
  3. Copy the SCRIPTS folder onto the SD card, merging with the one already there. Afterwards you should have SCRIPTS/TOOLS/NacoSim.lua and a folder SCRIPTS/NacoSim/.
  4. Eject the card, then open NacoSim from the radio's Tools menu.

To update, copy the new SCRIPTS folder over the old one. Your settings are kept; if a new version changes how settings are stored, it migrates yours.

Memory: use a light model

NacoSim is a big script for a radio. EdgeTX gives scripts a small heap, and how much is left depends on the model that is active: a model set up for a real receiver (ELRS with telemetry) leaves less than an empty one. On a T14, NacoSim ran from an empty model but stopped at start-up with NacoSim.lua:44 not enough memory when an ELRS model was selected.

  • Select an empty model first (no module, no telemetry sensors, few mixes: a "SIM" model you create for it works) and run NacoSim from there. Switch back to your flying model afterwards.
  • Still out of memory? Run NacoMem from the Tools menu on the model you use. It reports what each part of NacoSim costs and how much heap is left, on screen and in LOGS/nacomem.txt. Send us that file with your radio and EdgeTX version.
  • LOGS/nacosim_boot.txt says how far NacoSim got before it stopped.

We are working on making it lighter so that it fits next to an ELRS model too.

Flying

  • Sticks are read as set up in your model. Bring the throttle to zero to arm (THR DOWN is shown until you do), then take off.
  • ENTER respawns the quad. EXIT leaves the sim.
  • MENU (or a PAGE key, or a tap on a touch radio) opens the settings. The quad pauses while it is open.
  • A race is 3 laps by default, timed from your first pass through gate 1 to the pass that completes the last lap. The race clock is top right, the last lap bottom right. A beep marks each gate and a long beep the finish, followed by a result screen with the total and every lap.
  • A yellow diamond marks the next gate when it is on screen, with a short arrow along the way through it. When the gate is off to the side or behind you, an arrow at the edge of the screen points to it. If you are on the wrong side of the gate, so that flying at it would cross it backwards and not count, the marker gets an X.
  • Gates are drawn and timed 25% bigger than the map says, to make them easier to hit on a small screen. Flags pass-width is doubled, too.

Tracks

  • Oval — a simple eight-gate loop to learn the sim on.
  • Race vibes — the sim's own track: ten gates and three flags.
  • Macabim — the Velocidrone track Macabim 2027 FRIL Race 2: 15 gates and 10 flags in a tight field.
  • MGP Qualifier 2024 1, 2 and 3 — the MultiGP 2024 qualifier tracks: 10 to 12 gates and 5 flags each, about 160 to 180 m a lap.
  • MGP Micro 2024 — the 2024 MultiGP spring micro qualifier: 14 gates and 4 flags in a small field, about 70 m a lap.

A flag is a pole you pass on one side, in the right direction; the pennant on it points to the side to pass. Flags are forgiving: you do not need to be close, and clipping the wrong side of the pole by a metre still counts.

The radio sim flies upright gates and flags only. Dive gates and cube openings are left out (the MultiGP qualifiers have none) and obstacles such as posts and barriers are not modelled, so these are the courses, not the full tracks. Gate sizes are estimates. On the MultiGP tracks the way each gate is flown and the side to pass each flag are worked out from the course layout, not read from the game, so a gate or flag may be the wrong way round compared with the real track: tell us if you see one.

Want your own? A track is one text file; copy it into SCRIPTS/NacoSim/maps and it shows up in the Map setting. The format is in the README inside the zip.

Settings

  • Sound — gate and lap beeps on or off.
  • Map — the track: Oval (built in), Race vibes and Macabim, the MultiGP 2024 qualifiers and more. Each track is a small file in SCRIPTS/NacoSim/maps.
  • Laps — 1, 3, 5 or 10.
  • Tilt — camera up-tilt, 0 to 60 degrees.
  • Rates — presets (Quad, BF def, Soft) or Edit rates to set rate, super rate and expo for roll, pitch and yaw. The bottom line shows the resulting maximum deg/s per axis. Type in the rates you fly on your real quad and the sticks feel the same.
  • Info — radio, EdgeTX version, screen size and speed.

Settings are saved on the SD card when you close the menu.

Helping test on another radio

If you try it on a radio other than a T14, please open Settings → Info and note what it says, then tell us:

  • Does it start, and does the picture fit your screen with nothing cut off?
  • Do MENU, the wheel or keys, ENTER and EXIT all work? Which key opens the settings?
  • Is it smooth? Info shows frames per second and milliseconds per frame.
  • Which model was active, empty or an ELRS one? If it ran out of memory, send LOGS/nacomem.txt from NacoMem.
  • Anything that looks wrong or stops.

Two files on the SD card help find problems: LOGS/nacosim.csv (a flight log) and LOGS/nacosim_last.txt (the last state, and any error). Send them with your report to @nacofpv or open an issue on GitHub.

Colour radios use a separate drawing path that has only been tried against a simulated screen, not real hardware. Expect rough edges there, and please report them.
