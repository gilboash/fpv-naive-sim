An FPV trainer you can fly anywhere

naco.fpvsim is a free FPV simulator built on one idea: the stick feel has to be right before anything else. Plug in your own radio and fly a 5″ racing quad in the browser, or load a pocket version onto the radio itself and fly on its own screen, with nothing else needed.

The web sim

Put your radio in USB Joystick mode, plug it in, and fly. The flight model has rotors, motors, battery sag and a Betaflight-style PID loop, and it is checked against Blackbox logs from real quads; you can load your own rates and tune from a log so the sticks feel the way yours do.

  • Race tracks with gates, flags and cubes, with lap timing and a result screen.
  • Your own tracks, written as plain data and kept in your browser.
  • Motor sound, an on-screen display and a fullscreen view, including on a phone.

NacoSim for your radio

A small rate-mode quad simulator that runs as a script on an EdgeTX radio. It draws a wireframe view on the radio's own screen and reads your real sticks and your own rates, so you can practise gates and laps anywhere, with no computer or cable.

  • Tested on a Jumper T14 and a RadioMaster TX16S, black and white or colour.

  • A lap timer, gate beeps, and a marker that tells you which way through a gate.

About the project

It is built in the open and checked against the real thing wherever that is possible. The model's timing is compared with logs from real quads, and where it still falls short, that gap is written down rather than hidden. The web sim runs entirely in your browser. It sends this server a session summary (your settings, the maps you flew, how long, and a random ID), and there is a switch in its Settings to turn that off. Separately, the site counts visits to its pages and downloads of the radio package: it keeps your country and a one-way hash of your address, not the address itself, so repeat visits count once.

Made by @nacofpv. Open source on GitHub.
