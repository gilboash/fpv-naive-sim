1 · Device

Put the radio in USB Joystick mode, plug it in, then move a stick or press a button — browsers hide gamepads until they see activity.

2 · Channel mapping

The presets are a guess and often a wrong one: EdgeTX applies the stick mode in the radio and emits AETR, so both the axis numbers and the invert flags may not match yours. Use Detect per channel — it reads the direction you actually moved, which is the only thing that is reliably right. Check pitch in particular: forward stick must fly you forward.

Switches

Bind arm and reset to switches and the whole session runs from the radio. Arm is held on, exactly as on a flight controller — flick it off in the air and you disarm in the air. Reset fires once on the flick, so holding it does not respawn over and over. A switch that is already on when the page loads will not arm until you flick it off once, which is the same guard a real quad has.

Sound

Motor noise synthesised from the model's own rotor speeds — one tone per motor at blade-pass frequency, so it beats and swells the way a real quad does under load, and impacts you can hear. There is nothing to download; it is generated as you fly. Turned off, it is exactly as before: the audio graph is closed rather than muted, so nothing runs at all.

Improving the simulator

This sends a short summary to the machine hosting this page — which maps you flew and for how long, your rates and PIDs, lap times and crash count. It is used to improve the simulator and goes nowhere else. No inputs, no positions and no recording of the flight itself; you are identified by a random id, which Reset everything below erases.

Reset everything

Puts every setting back to its default — channel mapping and calibration, rates, PIDs and filters, camera and map — and erases your pilot id, so the next session is a new pilot as far as the host is concerned. It only affects this browser, and it cannot be undone. The page reloads afterwards.

Diagnostics — raw axes and loop timing

Development instrumentation, kept because it is how the input path was measured in the first place. Raw input is worth opening if a channel is mapped to the wrong axis; the jitter test is a 60 s measurement of loop pacing and nothing to do with how the quad flies.

Flight recorder

Captures a flight in the same field layout Betaflight Blackbox uses, so a sim flight and a real log can be read through one path. Sampling happens inside the 1 kHz tick; the export happens off it.

Polling jitter

Measures the interval between physics-loop ticks on the main thread, how late each tick fired, and how often the radio actually reports new data. Keep this tab focused — a background tab is throttled and the numbers become meaningless.

1 · Stick check

Driven by your sticks, not by the flight model, so it answers one question: are the channels mapped the right way round? Push right and it should bank right; push forward and the nose should drop. Throttle does not move it — it hangs in space — but it does spin the props, so that channel can be checked too.

2 · Rates

The rate curve is most of what makes muscle memory transfer, so it has to be your curve. Every value here lives in the header of any Blackbox log — load one and it comes across, PIDs and filters included, rather than being retyped from a configurator. Saved in this browser.

3 · PIDs and filters

The gains the model flies on. Loading a Blackbox log in Settings → Rates brings these across too, which is the reliable way to fly your own tune. Changes apply about a second after you stop typing — sooner would rebuild the controller on every keystroke and jolt the quad mid-air.

4 · Response

What the controller is doing with those gains: commanded rate against achieved rate, and the motor outputs the mixer is asking for. The gap between the two is the tune.
